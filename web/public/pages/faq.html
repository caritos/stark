STARK

Frequently Asked Questions

Where is my data stored?
In Apple Calendar and Reminders on your device, which Apple syncs through iCloud if you use it. Events and reminders you add in Stark are created there, in the default calendar and list you choose in Settings > Apple Calendar and Reminders, and editing, completing or deleting them in Stark changes them there. Stark needs full access to Calendar and Reminders and explains why before iOS asks. A small file of Stark's own on the device keeps what Apple cannot hold: planner blocks for Apple reminders (and any attended or skipped marks saved on Apple events by earlier versions). There is no account and Stark itself uploads nothing. The day export (today and yesterday) and the Weekly Review share include the Apple items they show, and go only where you send them.

What about the items I made in Stark before?
Earlier versions of Stark kept them in standard iCalendar (.ics) files in Stark's private storage and could copy them into Apple Calendar and Reminders once. Stark now shows only Apple Calendar and Reminders and no longer reads these old files. They remain on the phone, untouched, as a backup, and are deleted only when you delete the app. Completed copies of repeating reminders made in Stark were not copied into Apple, so that history is only in the old files.

Does Stark sync between my devices?
Your Apple Calendar and Reminders items sync through iCloud if you use iCloud for them; that is Apple's syncing, and your other devices see what you add or change in Stark. Stark's old backup files do not sync; they are included in your normal device backups (iCloud Backup or a computer backup). Planner blocks stay on the device where you made them.

Which items can Stark change?
Anything in a calendar or list you can change. Items on calendars you cannot change (such as holidays or a calendar shared with you read-only) and events you were invited to are shown read-only; open them in Calendar or Reminders from Stark.

How do I add a recurring reminder or event?
Tap + to add an item, then use the Repeat row to choose daily, weekly, monthly or yearly, or a custom rule such as every two weeks or the first Tuesday of the month. Repeat End stops the series after a date or a number of times. A monthly item on day 29, 30 or 31, or a yearly one on February 29, is skipped in months without that day (that is how Apple Calendar and Reminders repeat); choose "Last day of the month" to land on the last day instead. A repeat Stark cannot show (made in Apple's apps) reads “Custom, set in Calendar” (or Reminders) and is left as it is.

What happens when I complete a repeating reminder?
It works as in Apple's Reminders: the reminder moves to its next date, and Apple keeps a separate completed copy for each completion. If it was overdue, Done records one completion and moves it to its next date after now; missed dates in between are not recorded. Reminders have no "skip this occurrence". Reopening a completed copy does not move the reminder back.

What happens to a reminder I miss?
An overdue reminder moves to today's section with its missed date and how many days overdue it is, until you complete it. A repeating reminder shows as a single open row.

Can I skip one occurrence of a repeating event?
Yes. Open the event and tap Remove This Occurrence; that one date is deleted from Apple Calendar and the rest of the series stays. Editing or deleting a repeating item changes the whole series.

What happened to attended and skipped marks on events?
Stark no longer records them: the event screen has no attended or skipped buttons. Marks saved by earlier versions stay in Stark's small file on the device but are not shown.

What about reminders without a date?
They are not in the agenda. They appear in Search and in the planner's NO DATE pane; once you plan one onto a day, it appears in that day's agenda. Opening one shows "No date" and an Add Date button, which sets today (all day).

Can I search old items?
Yes. Search looks through the titles, notes and locations of every open Apple reminder in the lists you show, and the Apple events and completed reminders from two years before to two years after today. A repeating Apple event is one row.

Can I tag people, projects, or labels in a task?
Yes — just type the tag anywhere in the title or notes. +project and @context are todo.txt's own standard tags; %label and ~person are free-form ones Stark also understands, for whatever categories or people you want to track (e.g. "Call mom ~mom %family"). As you type a tag, Stark suggests matching ones you've already used in the items it has loaded, Apple items included, so you don't have to remember your own spelling.

How do I plan my day?
Tap the calendar-clock button next to TODAY or TOMORROW in the agenda to open Plan. Drag tasks from the tray onto the timeline, resize a block with its bottom edge, then tap the check to save. The tray includes your Apple reminders (from the lists you show) and a NO DATE pane for reminders without a date. Planning never changes a task's due date: a task you do not finish keeps its original due date. The arrows in the header step to another day, so a whole week can be planned, and past days are shown view only with what got done and what was missed.

What does the Weekly Review count?
Completed and still-open reminders for the week. Reminders count in the week they were completed.

Can I turn an event into a task?
Yes. Open the event, use the Event / Reminder switch at the top, and tap the check. The event's end time is dropped, its location moves into the task's notes and its URL becomes the reminder's own link. It works the other way round too; a task's priority is dropped when it becomes an event. For an Apple item Stark creates the other kind and deletes the original, so its alerts, invitees and any occurrences edited one by one are lost; Stark asks first when the event has invitees (they may be notified), and an item whose repeat Stark cannot copy cannot be converted.

Does Stark work offline?
Yes. Stark never needs a connection: everything is read from and written to your device. If you use iCloud for Calendar and Reminders, Apple syncs your changes when you are back online.
